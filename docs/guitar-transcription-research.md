# Guitar transcription examples: notation and layout

Research scope: visually inspected the transcribers' own linked PDF pages, not just search snippets. These are examples of editorial practice; their contents are not automatically licensed training data or proven ground truth for our audio excerpts.

## Examples inspected

1. **Grant Green, “Blues for Willarene,” from *Grant’s First Stand*.** [Bruce Saunders's transcription](https://www.brucesaunders.com/Resources/Blues%20for%20Willarene%20Grant%20Green%20solo.pdf), pages 1 and 3. A single treble staff, explicitly identified as concert pitch with guitar sounding an octave below; 4/4 with a key signature; generally four bars per system; chord symbols above the bars. The melody uses many eighths, quarter notes, rests, ties and some explicit triplets. Boxed timestamps and numbered choruses connect portions of the transcription to the recording. This is a visual reference for presentation, not a note-level label for our other Grant Green takes.
2. **Wes Montgomery, “Four on Six,” live *Smokin' at the Half Note*.** [Aaron Germain's transcription](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5ffbe904a24aef1e5b95301c/t/600380ccdea472688fa74908/1610842316104/Wes%2BMontgomery%2Bs%2Bsolo%2Bon%2BFour%2Bon%2BSix.pdf), pages 1 and 3. Source album and transcriber are named, with tempo, key signature, four-bar systems, harmonic symbols and clear rhythmic groupings. The later page explicitly notates simultaneous octave dyads and chords. This is a **different performance** from our *Incredible Jazz Guitar* recording and must not be used as an aligned note reference for it.
3. **Wes Montgomery, “Four on Six,” *The Incredible Jazz Guitar of Wes Montgomery*.** [Peter Martin's transcription](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5cc71b1eb10f257bd04b072f/t/5dfaab36dd325c224e996f36/1576708920034/Four%2Bon%2BSix%2B-%2BWes%2BMontgomery%2B-%2BMelody%2Band%2BSolo%2B-%2BScore.pdf), pages 1–4. Its title identifies the same album as our test and gives a quarter-note tempo of approximately 200, compared with about 103 estimated for our 20-second excerpt. This suggests a half-tempo ambiguity, not proof of correct phase or a verified transcription. The visual score is piano-adapted: grand staff and rhythmic-slash accompaniment rather than guitar TAB; it organizes the solo into choruses with four-bar systems. Check the actual remaster and timestamp alignment before using its harmony or note positions.
4. **Joe Pass, “Night and Day.”** [Monte Moore's transcription](https://www.guitardaily.net/uploads/1/2/3/2/12329041/night_and_day_1.pdf), pages 1 and 3. Standard notation is paired with a synchronized six-line TAB staff, chord symbols and bar numbers. This PDF identifies Joe Pass but does not establish the exact album/take on the inspected title page; do not assert it matches our *Virtuoso* excerpt. A separately indexed [Pere Soto transcription](https://peresoto.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/night-and-day-transcription-Full-Score.pdf) describes itself as the *Virtuoso I* recording, but the origin returned HTTP 404 during this check, so it is not a dependable accessible reference.

## Implications for the current system

- **Fix the pulse before styling.** A tracker can select a pulse at half or double the musically intended tempo; a forced eighth grid can then misrepresent durations or discard attacks. Offer explicit tempo and beat/downbeat overrides, identify likely octave ambiguities and validate against the correct recording. A transcription cannot be judged by the proportion of eighth notes alone.
- **Treat lead sheets as rhythmic notation, not raw waveform timing.** Swing may be implied by even-looking eighths, while straight figures, sixteenths, genuine rests and explicitly marked triplets remain available when the evidence supports them. Do not invent triplets or alter the melody solely to make it look neater.
- **Prefer a consistent, generous score layout.** Four bars per system is common in these examples, with clear chord symbols, key/time signature, measure numbers and the identity of the source take. A compact 20-second machine draft may have fewer systems, but wide blank regions do not by themselves imply wrong notation. Do not synthesize chord changes without a reliable separately sourced chart or user input.
- **Single-line and polyphonic guitar require different modes.** The Wes source preserves octaves and chords, while Joe Pass solo guitar can combine bass, melody and harmony. Current single-voice selection discards simultaneous notes by design; a visually polished lead sheet cannot recover that lost musical information. Keep raw polyphonic MIDI, add a distinct polyphonic workflow, and avoid claiming a single line is the entire performance.
- **TAB requires fingering evidence or a labeled suggestion.** Multiple string/fret positions can yield the same pitch; notation alone cannot prove the guitarist's fingering. Do not fabricate TAB or imply it matches an artist's physical fingering without evidence.
- **Benchmark the exact performance.** Independently time-align the same recording/take to a suitable note-level reference and separately score onset, pitch, duration and overlapping notes. Do not substitute an alternate live version, or use a source meant only for visual comparison as a verified ground truth.
